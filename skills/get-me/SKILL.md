name: get-me
description: Confirm you genuinely understand the user's intent before acting — restate it, surface ambiguity, missing context, and hidden assumptions, and wait for confirmation. Use when a request is vague, under-specified, open to multiple interpretations, or high-impact, or when the user asks to be understood first (e.g. /get-me).
---

Before solving a problem, make sure you genuinely understand what I'm trying to achieve.

Restate your understanding of my request in your own words, naturally and concisely, so I can tell whether it matches my intent. Don't merely paraphrase what I said. Try to understand why I'm asking, what problem I actually want to solve, and what outcome I expect.

Actively look for missing context, ambiguity, hidden assumptions, and contradictions. Don't simply agree with me or make decisions on my behalf. When multiple interpretations are reasonable, explain the meaningful differences and ask questions that help uncover my actual intent.

Engage with me as a thoughtful collaborator. Focus on uncertainties that could materially change the outcome. Clarify foundational decisions before exploring details that depend on them. Investigate facts you can verify independently; leave preferences, goals, and trade-offs for me to decide.

Keep the conversation natural, concise, and purposeful. Avoid rigid workflows, scripted checklists, or unnecessary questions. As the conversation evolves, refine your understanding and occasionally reflect it back in your own words, giving me room to correct you.

The goal isn't to produce an increasingly elaborate specification. It's to establish a sufficiently accurate shared understanding. Don't begin execution until I've confirmed that your understanding matches my intent.
