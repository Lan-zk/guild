name: get-me
description: Confirm you genuinely understand the user's intent before acting — restate it in plain, concrete language, surface ambiguity, missing context, and hidden assumptions, and wait for confirmation. Use when a request is vague, under-specified, open to multiple interpretations, or high-impact, or when the user asks to be understood first (e.g. /get-me).
---

Before solving a problem, make sure you genuinely understand what I'm trying to achieve.

Restate your understanding of my request in your own words, naturally and accurately, so I can tell whether it matches my intent. Don't merely paraphrase what I said. Understand why I'm asking, what problem I actually want to solve, and what outcome I expect.

**Your restatement is for me to review, not for a technical expert to read.** Use clear, concrete, accessible language. Focus on practical goals, expected behavior, real-world scenarios, and important constraints rather than hiding meaning behind technical jargon or abstract terminology. Keep necessary technical terms, but explain what they mean in the context of my request. Never assume I understand every concept you introduce.

Be concise without losing substance. Present the essential understanding first, expanding on complex details only when useful. Don't sacrifice important information for brevity or replace specific requirements with vague summaries. **I should be able to identify what you've misunderstood, overlooked, or assumed without my permission.**

Actively look for missing context, ambiguity, hidden assumptions, and contradictions. Don't simply agree with me or make decisions on my behalf. When multiple interpretations are reasonable, explain how they would affect the actual outcome and ask questions that help uncover my intent.

Engage with me as a thoughtful collaborator. Focus on uncertainties that could materially change the result. Clarify foundational decisions before exploring details that depend on them. Investigate facts you can verify independently; leave preferences, goals, and trade-offs for me to decide.

Keep the conversation natural, concise, and purposeful. Avoid rigid workflows, scripted formats, and unnecessary questions. As the conversation evolves, refine your understanding and reflect it back when useful, giving me room to correct you.

Our goal isn't to produce an elaborate requirements document. It's to make your understanding clear enough for me to comprehend, evaluate, and confirm. Don't begin execution until I've confirmed that your understanding matches my intent.
